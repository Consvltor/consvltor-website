Photo Opportunities, Power Cuts

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin met in Shenzhen on 19 November and issued a four-point declaration that sets no timetable for a nuclear-risk dialogue. Overnight on 20 November a Russian attack forced Ukraine’s nuclear plants to cut output and left some consumers in Kyiv and ten regions without power. Tankers were hit again near Hormuz between 15 and 18 November, as reported by the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre. The leaders of the Quad, the security grouping of Australia, India, Japan and the United States, met in Manila on 17 November and welcomed an expansion of Australia’s contribution to their cable partnership to focus on electricity cables.

The common underwriting issue is, once more, continuity of service. Attacks in Ukraine and near Hormuz tested it on specific days. The Shenzhen declaration sets no implementation timetable or supply volumes, and the Quad statement gives no funding figure or delivery timetable for its cable work.

Macro and Geopolitical Landscape

The Federal Reserve’s October minutes showed a hawkish hold with two votes for a rise, UK inflation rose to 3.6% in October, the European Commission proposed a Ukraine loan of up to €45 billion for 2027, and Ukrainian strikes on 16 and 17 November damaged the Kerch Bridge.

The minutes of the Federal Open Market Committee’s October meeting, released on 18 November, show a hawkish hold: the committee voted 10 to 2 to keep the target range at 3.75% to 4.00%, two members preferred a 25 basis point increase, a few who backed the hold called it a close call, and almost all participants judged that further increases would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlooks.23 The Office for National Statistics reported that UK annual consumer price inflation rose to 3.6% in October from 3.5% in September.4 For borrowers with floating-rate debt or refinancing in the next two quarters, neither the Fed minutes nor rising UK inflation supports assuming rate relief in a base case. In the UK, the Competition and Markets Authority’s statutory deadline for its final decision on nexfibre’s acquisition of Substantial, which owns Netomnia, is 15 December, after the CMA provisionally found competition concerns on 2 October, and it published further submissions on 13 and 18 November.5

The European Commission proposed on 18 November a new loan of up to €45 billion to Ukraine for 2027, of which up to €25 billion could go to military aid and up to €20 billion to general budget support, against a funding gap of €70 billion; Ukraine would be required to repay the principal only once it receives reparations from Russia.6 EU leaders are expected to discuss the proposal at a special summit on 26 and 27 November, and the Commission said it was confident that all 27 member states would join, including the holdouts Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia.7 The loan is a proposal, and member states have not approved it. On 16 and 17 November Ukraine struck the Kerch Bridge, the road and rail link between Russia and Crimea; Russia’s Investigative Committee confirmed structural damage and traffic across it was suspended on 17 November; Crimea’s Russian-appointed governor said rail traffic was restored later that day, and the Kremlin said the bridge would be rebuilt.8910 Saudi Gazette, citing Anadolu, said a ferry crossing could only partly compensate because its capacity is very limited, and Al Jazeera quoted an analyst who judged the attack militarily counterproductive, which is one expert’s view.911

Ukraine: Grid Strikes and the Starshield Question

A Russian attack overnight and on the morning of 20 November damaged Ukrenergo substations, forced Ukraine’s nuclear plants to reduce output and left some consumers in Kyiv and ten regions without power, while allegations about Starshield terminal restrictions remain unresolved.

Ukraine’s energy ministry said that damage to electricity networks forced nuclear plants to reduce output after the large-scale overnight and morning attack on 20 November, and that output would return to normal once the networks were restored; two energy workers were injured in the Kharkiv region.12 Ukrinform reported that Ukrenergo substations were damaged, that emergency outages were imposed in the Poltava and Kharkiv regions and that Odesa had a large outage, and it said Russia was again targeting facilities that had just been restored.13 Ukrainska Pravda quoted First Deputy Energy Minister Artem Nekrasov saying that some consumers in Kyiv and ten regions were without power.14 The ministry’s figure describes affected consumers within those territories and not complete blackouts of them.

A separate issue concerns the defence of energy infrastructure. Zelensky said, in remarks reported by AP on 15 November, that Starshield limitations impaired interceptor drones and threaten the protection of energy infrastructure ahead of winter; AP reported his suspicion that SpaceX had imposed them but did not establish who imposed the restrictions or why.15 Al Jazeera set out on 16 November why the limits hurt Ukraine against jet-powered drones, and the Foundation for Defense of Democracies reported on 18 November that Kyiv still lacked a convincing explanation, while SpaceX and the Pentagon have denied any policy change.1617 Whether service was restricted, by whom and on what notice is not established in the sources reviewed.

For operators and investors with Ukrainian telecom, tower or data-centre assets, availability in the next fortnight is set by battery autonomy, generator fuel and the rotation of outage groups, with the ministry saying the nuclear plants will return to normal output once the networks are restored. Operators that rely on Starlink or Starshield-class terminals for backup backhaul should test the case in which service is restricted by a third party and price the cost of an alternative path accordingly.

Shenzhen: A Four-Point Declaration Without Dates

Trump, Xi and Putin met in Shenzhen on 19 November and issued a four-point declaration that reaffirms that a nuclear war cannot be won and promises a dialogue on nuclear risks, with no timetable and no formal arms-control negotiating process announced.

The three leaders met on 19 November, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, and released a four-point declaration. It commits the three countries to a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing the proliferation of nuclear weapons, but specifies no arms-reduction targets, negotiating framework or timetable.18 ABC News reported that the declaration says nothing about the war in Ukraine or the war with Iran, although those issues were discussed at the summit, and that the leaders reached no tangible diplomatic breakthroughs; its one energy item was a pledge to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products and to keep energy flows stable, without volumes or a timetable.19 Foreign Policy judged that the summit produced little of substance and described a statement, issued by Xinhua, that echoes themes Xi has promoted, among them opposition to Cold War thinking and to economic decoupling.20

Separately, the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) voted on 19 November to bar China Mobile, China Telecom and China Unicom from direct access to US phone numbers, citing national security and illegal robocalls.2122 The FCC action shows that national-security screening of telecom plumbing continues on its own calendar; the bar concerns direct access to numbering resources and does not by itself end all interconnection.

For operators and investors with US-China, Russia-linked or energy-trading exposure, the declaration contains no dated or quantified commitment that bears on infrastructure. Contracts, licences and supply arrangements keep their existing legal terms, apart from the new FCC rule, and the one energy item, a pledge on refined products, has no volume, date or mechanism attached in the reporting reviewed.

§7

Hormuz: Tankers Keep Getting Hit Near the Strait

The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre reported tankers hit near Hormuz between 15 and 18 November, India reported injured and missing crew from the 16 November attack on the MT Tara Gas, and Brent was above $90 on 17 November.

Seatrade Maritime reported on 16 November that an attack on a tanker in the Arabian Gulf on 15 November, as the vessel approached the strait, had been confirmed through the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) centre, which cited Indian shipping authorities; the initial report said the crew were safe.23 UKMTO also issued an alert on 16 November that a vessel had been struck by an unknown projectile on an outbound transit, with an engine room fire.23 India’s Ministry of External Affairs said on 17 November that the LPG tanker MT Tara Gas had been attacked in the Strait of Hormuz on 16 November: 21 of the 24 Indian nationals aboard were evacuated ashore at Khasab, two injured crew members were in hospital and one was missing.24 ADNOC Logistics & Services said on 18 November that one of its vessels had been attacked while transiting the strait the previous evening, leaving 11 crew injured and one missing, according to the Emirates News Agency.25 UKMTO also issued warnings on 17 and 18 November of tankers struck by projectiles north of Khasab, in one case with a crew member missing; the reports reviewed do not link these to the vessels described above.2627

Iranian naval forces, including the Revolutionary Guards Navy, have also claimed that mines hit tankers on 13, 14, 16 and 17 November.28293031 Seatrade said the vessels in the 16 November claims were unidentified and that the claims had not been independently verified. Those claims do not establish that mines caused the separately reported attacks. For underwriting, the point is that commercial vessels near the strait are being hit, whatever the weapon. Brent was above $90 on 17 November, according to engine.online.32

For operators and investors with Gulf data-centre, landing-station or cable exposure, war-risk premiums and repair-vessel access are the variables to price. Review force majeure definitions, war-risk cover and marine-access terms for any asset whose maintenance depends on vessels transiting the strait, and set a case in which vessels continue to be hit next to a case in which the hits stop.

§8

Quad Summit in Manila: Electricity Cables Join the Cable Partnership

The statement of the Quad leaders (Australia, India, Japan and the United States) of 17 November in Manila welcomes an expansion of Australia’s contribution to the cable partnership to focus on electricity cables, and welcomes upcoming undersea cable projects in the Indian Ocean.

The Quad is a security grouping of Australia, India, Japan and the United States. Its joint statement, issued in Manila on 17 November, notes progress under the Quad Partnership for Cable Connectivity and Resilience, which it says has expanded access to secure and trusted communications infrastructure.33 It welcomes an expansion of Australia’s contribution to focus on electricity cables, aimed at regional energy security and connectivity, and welcomes upcoming undersea cable projects in the Indian Ocean.33 The same text welcomes a new Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration, says patrol aircraft from the member countries are scheduled to take part in a maritime exercise in a Quad context for the first time in 2027, and commits to open, safe and resilient maritime transit corridors.33 Business Today described the outcome as a series of initiatives that reaffirm the centrality of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN).34

The statement gives no dollar figure for the cable work and names no project or route in the passages reviewed, so the scale and the timetable of the electricity-cable work are unknown from the text. That limits what can be priced today: the statement indicates that the partnership’s scope now includes power links as well as telecom cables, which could widen the set of projects that attract co-financing.

For developers and investors in Indian Ocean and Pacific cable routes, the useful step is to establish which projects sit inside the partnership’s pipeline and which party funds each one, because a welcome in a leaders’ statement does not itself commit capital. Treat Quad co-financing as an upside case with an identified counterparty and not as a base-case funding source, and review whether the surveillance and corridor commitments could affect route permissions near contested waters.
