Repairs, Permits & Power Cuts

Reuters reported on 23 October, citing two anonymous senior Iranian security officials, that Tehran has detailed plans to hit Gulf undersea cables if war resumes and would aim to prevent repairs. A Russian drone strike on the Dnipro railway station on 29 October killed 15 people, and Russian strikes that night left up to 350,000 people in the Dnipro region without power. Indonesia said it cut pipeline gas to Singapore by 31 million standard cubic feet a day from 24 October, and its digital minister said on 27 October that most cable routes landing in Singapore cross Indonesian waters. On 29 October the US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) voted to stop recognising test labs in China from December 2028 and announced a 19 November vote on barring Chinese carriers from direct access to US phone numbers.

The title points to three dependencies rather than three completed events: repairs, in Iranian officials’ reported contingency plan to obstruct the repair of Gulf cables; permits, in the FCC’s adopted lab-recognition rule, which takes effect in 2028, and its proposed restrictions on carrier access to US numbers; and power cuts, in the strikes around Dnipro. The reduction in pipeline gas to Singapore shows a related dependency on fuel supply, although Singapore’s regulator reports sufficient supply. For infrastructure investors, the right to restore, certify or connect a service may sit with a party other than the asset owner.

Macro and Geopolitical Landscape

European benchmark gas passed €100 a megawatt-hour on 28 October, its first time above that level since December 2022 according to Euronews, and the Federal Reserve held its main policy interest rate at 3.75% to 4.00% the same day on a 10-2 vote, with both dissenters favouring a rise.

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won Brazil’s runoff on 25 October with 51.8% of the vote, and on 26 October the Ibovespa closed down 13.9% at 176,608, according to the Rio Times, while Reuters reported that the index had fallen more than 15% in early trading, its largest daily percentage drop since 1998, with trading halted for 30 minutes, and that the real was down 8.3% at 5.4274 per dollar later in the session.78 Brazil’s Treasury cancelled its 27 October inflation-linked and 29 October fixed-rate bond auctions while keeping a Selic-linked sale.9 Flávio Bolsonaro has disputed the result, according to the Rio Times.7 For owners of towers, fibre and data centres in Brazil, the transmission is the currency and the cost of funding: dollar-denominated debt and imported equipment cost more in reais.

Gulf Cable Plans and Repair Access

Reuters reported on 23 October, citing two anonymous senior Iranian security officials, that Tehran has detailed plans to hit Gulf undersea cables if war resumes and would aim to prevent repairs, while TeleGeography put the share of international bandwidth crossing the Strait of Hormuz at under 1%.

One of the two officials told Reuters that Iran would aim to prevent repairs after attacks, which would prolong any outage, and the other said Iran has full access to the cables and that Gulf states should hold Washington responsible for any damage; neither gave detail on method or location.15 A deputy head of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said on 21 October that Iran could target seven major cables under Hormuz.16 Alan Mauldin of TeleGeography told Reuters that under 1% of international bandwidth transits Hormuz and that the Red Sea, which carries about 3% of global international demand and about 10% of inter-regional traffic, could be a more significant target.15 Neither the US State Department nor Iran’s foreign ministry responded to requests for comment. The report provides no corroboration beyond the two anonymous officials and does not establish that any attack has occurred, and Reuters added that higher insurance costs and delays in obtaining permits to enter territorial waters could complicate repairs.15

The plan arrived alongside military and diplomatic developments of different evidential weight. The Houthis claimed on 27 October a ballistic missile strike on the Ras Tanura refinery, which has a capacity of 550,000 barrels a day, and Saudi Arabia did not confirm it, while Argus and a Bloomberg source reported a fire near the export terminal that was contained.1718 The Wall Street Journal reported on 28 October, citing people briefed on the conversations, that Washington told several Gulf states to prepare for a short, intense military campaign after the 3 November US midterm elections.19 Reports of a letter from Vice President JD Vance to Mohsen Rezaei, a senior Iranian official, differ on when it was sent, whether Qatari mediators carried it and what Donald Trump intended by it, and its text is unpublished.19 Delegations from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey were in Tehran on 26 October for planned meetings with Iranian officials, AP reported, citing anonymous officials.20 Brent’s December contract settled at $116.72 on 23 October, its highest settlement since 29 April, and S&P Global counted 17 ships transiting Hormuz on each of 24 and 25 October.2122

For operators and investors with Gulf cable, landing-station or data-centre exposure, the variables worth pricing are repair access, route diversity and cover. A plan that targets repairs lengthens an outage, so service-level terms that assume restoration within weeks may warrant a stress test against an outage of months. Capacity routed through the Red Sea, which carries a larger share of international demand than Hormuz on TeleGeography’s figures, may warrant the same review, as may war and political-violence cover on repair-ship charters and landing stations.

Ukraine’s Grid and the Talks Calendar

A Russian jet-powered drone struck a train platform at Dnipro station on 29 October, killing 15 people by the toll the Kyiv Independent gave on 30 October, and Russian strikes that night left up to 350,000 people in the Dnipro region without power, according to the Kyiv Independent.

Ukraine’s Air Force said four jet-powered drones attacked at about 11:00 on 29 October and three were shot down, and President Zelensky called the Dnipro station strike a horrific attack.23 The first reports gave at least 14 dead, and the toll reached 15 on 30 October when a 40-year-old man died in hospital, with 11 of the wounded still hospitalised and six in serious condition.24 Governor Oleksandr Hanzha said 25,000 people lost electricity in the city and region during the daytime strikes, which also killed two 15-year-old girls in Chernihiv and at least three people in Kremenchuk, where Poltava Oblast declared 30 October a day of mourning.23 Ballistic missiles hit Kyiv after 22:00 and killed one person, and the report did not identify an energy-site hit in Kyiv, while the governor put the Dnipro region’s outage at up to 350,000 people after strikes on energy infrastructure.25 Zelensky said Russia has fired more than 100 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and its surroundings since 14 October, and the National Bank of Ukraine forecasts a 0.4% contraction this year, the first since 2022.2526

A local ceasefire around Oleshky in Kherson Oblast began on 27 October, announced by the White House on 23 October, and covers demining, aid delivery and voluntary evacuation until 1 November.2728 Ukraine’s ombudsman reported 30 hours without an incident.29 Kyiv Post notes that no independent monitor has been identified.28 Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said on 25 October that Moscow is ready to resume trilateral talks with Ukraine and the US after 4 November, and Zelensky said on 28 October that he expects a meeting in Abu Dhabi by 10 November, which would be the first US-mediated round since February.303123 Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk gave 4 November as the date of the first round, and Zelensky’s team did not confirm it.32 Zelensky has said Ukraine halted refinery strikes for three days after Trump announced an energy truce on 11 October and that forces have not been ordered to stop, while the Kyiv Independent reported on 29 October that the pace of strikes had slowed sharply and that no formal pause had been agreed.33

For operators and investors with assets in Ukraine or within reach of long-range strikes, the exposure that determines outcomes is power autonomy: hours of battery and generator supply at sites and data centres, fuel logistics and the time to restore grid supply after strikes on the scale seen around Dnipro. A local ceasefire on one front, with talks unscheduled and strikes continuing, may warrant planning for continued outages rather than for a negotiated pause. Issue 027 reported the loss of a Kyiv data centre on 19 October.

Indonesia and Singapore: Gas, Haze and Cable Routes

Indonesia said it cut pipeline gas to Singapore by 31 million standard cubic feet a day from 24 October, and on 27 October its digital minister said that most cable routes landing in Singapore cross Indonesian waters.

Indonesia’s energy minister, Bahlil Lahadalia, said on 26 October that the cut of 31 million standard cubic feet a day was about 8% of the 409.55 million a day exported by pipeline to Singapore.34 SKK Migas, Indonesia’s upstream oil and gas regulator, had announced reductions of 15 billion British thermal units a day from Natuna and 16 billion from Sumatra,35 and Singapore’s Energy Market Authority (EMA) put the reduction at 17 billion British thermal units a day over 18 days, less than 2% of daily gas demand of more than 1,000 billion, with importers pursuing recourse under their contracts and its gas company ready to import more liquefied natural gas.36 The published volumes are not reconciled. EMA said that reductions of 20 to 100 billion British thermal units a day in recent months, from maintenance, were replaced without incident and that power generators are required to hold fuel reserves, including diesel.36 An 18-day reduction from 24 October would run to about 11 November.

Singapore’s Energy Market Authority published its five-year energy infrastructure plan on 26 October, with five new gas-fired plants over 2026 to 2031 adding about 26% to the current 12 gigawatts of generation capacity, and liquefied natural gas regasification capacity rising from 10 million to 15 million tonnes a year when the second terminal opens by 2030; natural gas generates about 95% of Singapore’s electricity.37 On the Indonesian side, Bahlil said on 26 October that Indonesia is still evaluating electricity exports to other countries, without naming Singapore, and that he doubted they would happen in the near future, citing large domestic demand.38 The pipeline reduction is limited and short, the supply plan is medium-term, and the prospect of Indonesian electricity exports is uncertain.

Calvin Cheng, a former nominated member of parliament and a private citizen, posted an infographic on X before 27 October stating that 90% of Indonesia’s international internet traffic transits Singapore, captioned with a single word asking whether to cut it, and added that Singaporeans are civilised, law-abiding people who would obviously not do so.39 An Indonesian communications ministry director, Denny Setiawan, had said on 8 June that about 90% of Indonesia’s traffic still depends on Singapore-linked routes.39 Communications and Digital Minister Meutya Hafid replied on Instagram on 27 October that most cable routes landing in Singapore transit Indonesian waters, that a cut, whether by accident or sabotage, would threaten 80% of Singapore’s capacity, and that digital infrastructure is a sector to be protected jointly, for global connectivity as well as for the two countries.39 Katadata gives no method for the 80% figure and records no Singapore government response.39 The haze dispute runs in parallel: Reuters described the fires as Indonesia’s worst in 11 years, Singapore’s National Environment Agency served notices under the Transboundary Haze Pollution Act on 23 October on 10 Singapore-registered companies, and on 28 October Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong, Minister K Shanmugam and Minister Grace Fu met their senior leaders, with amendments to the Act planned for the next sitting of Parliament.404142

For owners of Singapore cable landings and data-centre capacity, the exposures to price are gas contract terms and the cost of replacing pipeline gas with liquefied natural gas, and the share of landing capacity that depends on one corridor. The remarks on cutting cables came from a private citizen and from a minister replying to him, and the Katadata report records no official proposal to cut cables, so the exposure to review is the concentration of capacity in one corridor. Singapore operators with power purchase agreements indexed to gas may wish to check pass-through terms and the volume of liquefied natural gas that can be called on at short notice.

US-China Separation: Labs, Numbers and Model Exports

The FCC voted unanimously on 29 October to stop recognising test labs in China from December 2028, and announced a 19 November vote on barring China Mobile, China Telecom and China Unicom from direct access to US phone numbers.

The FCC voted to bar test labs and certification bodies in countries that deny reciprocal treatment to US labs, which includes China, and Chairman Brendan Carr said that about 82% of testing for devices seeking FCC authorisation was done in China in 2025, against under 4% in US labs.4344 The three serving Republican commissioners voted unanimously, the prohibitions are slated to take effect in December 2028, and the FCC streamlined approvals for devices tested in US labs or in labs in countries it does not consider national security risks.4443 The FCC also said on 29 October that it will vote on 19 November on a proposal to bar China Mobile, China Telecom, China Unicom and other Internet Protocol (IP)-enabled providers from direct access to US phone numbers, and that it plans to bar providers that use equipment from companies on its list of national security risks, such as Huawei and ZTE, with barring connections to US networks and operating data centres in the US under consideration.45 China’s foreign ministry said on 30 October that it firmly opposes the likely ban and will take necessary measures.45 The South China Morning Post noted that Trump is due to meet Xi Jinping at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit within weeks.46

China’s Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology published a draft revision on 16 October that would add 23 artificial intelligence (AI) technologies, covering models, agents and computing clusters, to its catalogue of technologies restricted from export, and the ministry said most AI technologies would remain exportable with simplified licensing for open-source exports.4748 A social media account affiliated with state broadcaster China Central Television said on 22 October that DeepSeek and MiniMax had won approval for global use of their models, and neither company had replied to Reuters when it first reported the post on 23 October.48 Reuters reported on 28 October, citing two unnamed people, that ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) plans to start limited production of HBM3E, a generation of high-bandwidth memory, within two months, with usable-chip yields of 20% to 30% against about 70% at SK Hynix.49 SK Hynix said on 28 October that it expects memory demand to exceed supply through 2030.50

For operators and investors, the exposures worth pricing are certification lead time and cost for devices and network equipment now tested in China, interconnection arrangements with Chinese carriers or providers that use equipment on the FCC’s list, and memory supply for data-centre builds. Handset, customer-premises equipment and network equipment makers could map which products rely on China-based labs and what capacity exists in reciprocal economies before December 2028.
