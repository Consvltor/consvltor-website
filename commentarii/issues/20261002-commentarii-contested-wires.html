CommentariiIssue 0242 October 2026 TMT & Digital Infrastructure

Contested Wires

Russia struck Kyivstar’s headquarters in Kyiv and data centres, including one that its defence ministry said belonged to Vodafone Ukraine, a claim not independently confirmed, on 27 September, after a week of strikes on the capital’s hosting and internet providers, and the Philippine Coast Guard drove the Chinese research ship Jia Hai Ke 7 out of waters near Itbayat on 1 October, citing concern over survey data from Luzon Strait cable corridors. Turkey issued NAVTEX 0953/26 for the research vessel Oruç Reis to survey the Anamur-Teknecik gas pipeline route to northern Cyprus until 25 October, and a Houthi drone put a transformer at Madinah’s Taibah power station out of service on 29 September, the Saudi-led coalition said, while US-Iran talks stalled. Each of the four situations concerns a data cable, a power line or a pipeline, and in each a state or an armed group is contesting who may survey it, supply it or strike it.

Macro and Geopolitical Landscape

The G7 agreed on 2 October to release up to 100 million barrels of oil over four months, with a frontloaded diesel release in the first 20 days.

The Group of Seven announced a coordinated release of strategic oil reserves through the International Energy Agency of up to 100 million barrels over four months, with a substantial diesel release in the first 20 days and a commitment to convene on further diesel releases if needed.67 Brent crude stood near $103 a barrel on 2 October, about 7.6% higher over the month, according to Trading Economics.8 For digital infrastructure the relevant channel is the cost of power and of money, since both feed into the return hurdles for power-intensive and long-dated assets.

The European Central Bank (ECB) raised its three key interest rates by 25 basis points on 10 September, taking the deposit facility rate to 2.50%, and its staff project euro-area headline inflation of 3.0% in 2026 and 2.5% in 2027, with the Governing Council saying the conflict in the Middle East continues to generate inflation pressure.9 Germany’s preliminary September inflation reading was 3.3%, with energy prices 14.9% above a year earlier, up from 10.5% in August, and core inflation at 2.4%.10 Operators and investors with euro or sterling debt maturing in the next 12 months may wish to test refinancing cases against a deposit rate of 2.50% and energy-driven inflation, and the G7 release may ease fuel costs without changing the rate path. Hyperoptic’s end-November maturity shows how a single refinancing window can set the timetable for a network.

§5

Kyiv Under Strike

Russia struck Kyivstar’s headquarters in Kyiv and data centres, including one it said belonged to Vodafone Ukraine, a claim not independently confirmed, on 27 September.

On 27 September Russia struck the Kyiv headquarters of Kyivstar, Ukraine’s largest mobile operator, and data centres belonging to internet providers, and Russia’s defence ministry said it had hit a data centre belonging to Vodafone Ukraine.11 President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said a Kyiv data centre was struck twice that morning, wounding two children and an adult, and Kyivstar reported no casualties at its headquarters.11 Ukraine’s Digital Ministry put the number of households in Kyiv and surrounding regions affected by outages at about 100,000, and Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said Russia had targeted major data centres to disrupt the flow of information.11 The sources reviewed carry no independent confirmation of the claim.

The strikes followed others earlier in the week. Kyiv Link, Pautina, Utels and Crazy Network reported outages after strikes on data centres around 23 September, and the Ministry of Digital Transformation cited about 100,000 households for that outage; Utels restored most customers within about two hours.1213 The same figure appears in reporting on both occasions, and the sources reviewed do not say whether it is a repeated estimate or two separate counts. Cosmonova, a Kyiv data centre operator, reported being hit on 25 and 26 September, and the internet provider Kolo.TV reported a third strike on 26 September, in both cases according to the companies’ own posts.14 Utels said a facility housing core network equipment lost power, and Crazy Network cited a strike on a central internet traffic exchange facility.12

For operators and investors, the exposure worth pricing is concentration. A single data centre or exchange point that carries a city’s traffic, the power autonomy of each node and the time needed to restore service after a site is lost determine how long an outage lasts. Hosting and exchange assets within reach of long-range strikes may warrant underwriting for site separation, backup power and restoration times.

§6

A Survey Ship off Itbayat

The Philippine Coast Guard drove the Chinese research ship Jia Hai Ke 7 out of waters about 37 nautical miles northwest of Itbayat, Batanes, on 1 October, after detecting it on 28 September.

The Philippine Coast Guard detected Jia Hai Ke 7 on 28 September through dark vessel detection, about 37 nautical miles northwest of Itbayat in the Batanes islands, and described its activity as illegal marine scientific research without government authorisation.15 Philstar reports that the ship’s automatic identification system (AIS) falsely placed it in Papua New Guinea shortly after it left China.15 A China Coast Guard vessel, CCG-2503, arrived and radio-challenged the Philippine cutter BRP Teresa Magbanua, and the research ship left at 11:42 on 1 October.15 Rear Admiral Jay Tarriela said the coast guard was concerned about data the vessel may have collected from undersea cable corridors in the Luzon Strait, which he described as part of the country’s critical infrastructure.15 Beijing described the Philippine action as harassment, according to the South China Morning Post.16

The reporting reviewed identified no cable fault or act of interference, and the sources describe unauthorised survey activity and a concern about what data was collected. Detailed seabed survey data can have legitimate scientific and commercial uses, and it can also improve understanding of cable approaches and potential vulnerabilities, which is why Manila’s concern carries weight in a strait between the Philippines and Taiwan. The sources reviewed do not say whether other survey vessels are working in the area. Cable owners and landing-party operators on Luzon Strait systems may wish to confirm what vessel-monitoring and notification arrangements apply near corridors and landing points, and what their repair contracts assume about access in contested waters.

§7

Survey Season off Cyprus

Turkey issued NAVTEX 0953/26 for the research vessel Oruç Reis to survey the Anamur-Teknecik gas pipeline route to northern Cyprus until 25 October.

Turkey issued the navigational warning NAVTEX 0953/26 on 1 October for the Oruç Reis, working with the support vessels Denar Pathfinder, Denar Echo and Kırıcı, to survey the planned Anamur-Teknecik pipeline route between southern Turkey and northern Cyprus until 25 October.1718 Turkish Minute reports that the Republic of Cyprus filed a complaint at the United Nations in August saying the project would violate its sovereignty and maritime rights without its consent.18

The Great Sea Interconnector, the Greece-Cyprus-Israel power cable, is a separate project, and the sources reviewed do not place the pipeline survey on its route. Turkey has recently demanded that work on the cable be coordinated with Ankara in areas it regards as its continental shelf, which Greece rejects.18 Issue 023 reported Greek plans for a French-flagged survey of the cable, and as of Sigmalive’s report of 21 September the survey had not started and its NAVTEX had not been issued, and that is the latest report located.19 Overlapping Turkish and Greek-French survey windows would make a standoff more plausible, as the 2024 episode south of Kasos described in Issue 023 showed. For investors in interconnectors and cables that cross disputed waters, installation schedules may need to assume that vessels from competing claimants will work near each other.

§8

The Taibah Transformer

A Houthi drone put one transformer out of service at the Taibah electricity station in Madinah at 5:11 a.m. on 29 September, the Saudi-led coalition said.

The Saudi-led coalition’s spokesman, Major General Turki al-Maliki, said its investigation of the site and recovered drone wreckage concluded that the Houthis planned and carried out the attack on the Taibah electricity distribution station, which supplies the Prophet’s Mosque, and said one transformer was temporarily taken out of service while the wider electricity network was unaffected.2021 Yemen’s army then reported air strikes in Taiz and Saada.21 A World Health Organization report, covered on 28 September, recorded 809 new casualties, 164 of them deaths, between 19 and 26 September, and a total of 4,481 casualties including 838 deaths since 6 August, in the renewed fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed government forces.22

On the diplomatic track, Axios first reported, and US officials told the Associated Press, that Secretary of State Marco Rubio ordered Iran’s delegation to leave New York after weekend talks with Qatari mediators made little progress.23 Iran’s UN mission denied that account, saying the delegation left on a schedule it gave the State Department on 17 September, and Arab News describes the negotiations to end the war as at an impasse.24

A single transformer outage with no effect on the wider grid has a limited direct effect on telecom networks. The exposure lies in the pattern, since Saudi power infrastructure is within reach of Houthi drones, and a facility that relies on a single external feed could be more exposed to a comparable disruption. Operators in the Gulf may wish to check feed redundancy and on-site generation at critical facilities.

Strategy Actions

Immediate · 0 to 90 days
  • Consider testing site separation, backup power and restoration times for data centres and exchange points within reach of long-range strikes. Kyiv providers reported strikes on at least four dates between 23 and 27 September.
  • Consider reviewing vessel-monitoring arrangements and repair contracts for cable systems on Luzon Strait routes. Manila’s concern covers survey data from the corridors.
  • Consider re-running refinancing cases at the ECB’s 2.50% deposit rate, with extension scenarios for maturities in the next 12 months. Hyperoptic is negotiating an extension on more than £98m due in November.
Platform · 3 to 12 months
  • Consider modelling the possibility of vessel standoffs into schedules for cable and interconnector projects in the Eastern Mediterranean. The Turkish survey notice runs to 25 October, and Greek-French survey plans may fall in the same period.
  • Consider confirming dual power feeds and on-site generation at Gulf sites. The Madinah outage affected one transformer, and a site on a single external feed could be more exposed to a comparable event.
Portfolio · 12 to 24 months
  • Consider weighing UK altnet exposure against the CMA’s expected 15 December decision on nexfibre and Substantial and the effect of Ofcom’s ruling on incumbent discounting. Both change the competitive set and the exit routes.
