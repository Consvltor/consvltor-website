Macro and Geopolitical Landscape

China’s Ministry of Commerce extended the suspension of six October 2025 export-control measures to 10 January 2027 on 9 November, and the US Bureau of Industry and Security extended its own suspension of the Affiliates Rule to the same date.

China’s Ministry of Commerce, with the General Administration of Customs, announced on 9 November in Announcement No. 58 that it was extending to 10 January 2027 the suspension of six measures it had issued in October 2025. They cover five rare earths (holmium, erbium, thulium, europium and ytterbium), related processing technologies and equipment, certain lithium batteries, graphite anode materials, and the 0.1% de minimis rule for foreign-made products containing Chinese-origin rare earths.2 Separately, China’s State Council, through its tariff commission, extended for two months the suspension of an additional 24% tariff on US goods, with a 10% tariff remaining, and the transport ministry extended to 10 January 2027 the suspension of special port fees on US-linked vessels.7 April 2025 licensing for seven medium and heavy rare earths is unaffected.2

The US side moved in parallel. The Bureau of Industry and Security filed a notice on 5 November, effective 9 November, that extends to 10 January 2027 the suspension of the Affiliates Rule, which would extend export controls to entities that are 50% or more owned by listed parties.1 The two extensions leave the truce that followed the 28 September summit in place, but the date is a cliff and not a settlement. The price gap shows what remains: European spot dysprosium oxide was quoted at US$3,300 to US$4,200 per kilogram on 5 November against US$215 to US$220 in China, according to The Oregon Group.8 None of this week’s four tiles depends on these measures, and the item belongs in an underwriting file for any asset that buys rare-earth magnets, batteries or graphite.

Hormuz: Claimed Tanker Fires, an Oman Lane and Falling Brent

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said on 10 November that two oil tankers were burning in the Strait of Hormuz, a claim no maritime authority had confirmed in the reporting reviewed, while Iraq says it reached understandings with Washington on a lane near Oman and Brent fell about 16% in the week to 6 November.

The Revolutionary Guard said on 2 November that two tankers, one of them a supertanker, had hit mines, and on 4 November that four tankers had been destroyed by mines south of Hormuz; the second claim is carried as unconfirmed.910 On 10 November the Guards said two tankers on an unauthorised route had been stopped by explosions and left burning, and that one, a supertanker, had hit a mine; AFP and Tasnim carry the statement.1112 Straits.live notes that no maritime authority has confirmed the claim, so repetition across outlets adds reach and no independent evidence.13

Iraq’s foreign minister, Fuad Hussein, told Al Jazeera on 8 November that Baghdad had reached understandings with Washington to export crude along a shipping lane near Oman, and put the war’s cost to Iraq at about $60bn. Iraq exported about 3.5 million barrels per day in October against 2.65 million in September.14 The statement is the minister’s account, and no source reviewed describes a jointly confirmed or secured lane. A North Oil Company source told Kurdistan 24, without official confirmation, that exports through Ceyhan in Turkey had reached 500,000 barrels per day in early November.15

Brent fell 16% in the week to 6 November to a two-month low of $93.22, its steepest weekly fall since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, and traded at $93.75 intraday on 9 November after a session low of $92.80.16 The Nation Thailand reported that Dated Brent had dropped below the front-month futures contract, a sign of weakness in physical trade, and linked part of the selling to strong Hormuz flows.17 Straits.live, an AI-assisted secondary brief, put Brent at $91.77 at 17:43 UTC on 10 November and cited IMF PortWatch for three transits on 8 November against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 a day.13 The price and the traffic count are from different days and measure different things, since a vessel count is not an oil volume and one day is not a trend. They do not give one synchronous signal, so corridor reliability is better judged from sustained transit volumes, incident reporting and insurance terms than from the oil price.

For operators and investors with Gulf data-centre, landing-station or cable exposure, the useful variable is whether any lane stays open while claims of mine strikes continue, because further confirmed incidents could reverse some of the price decline. Review war-risk cover, force majeure clauses and repair-vessel access terms against a strait where mine claims have continued while diplomacy runs.

Ukraine: Abu Dhabi Talks and the Substation Strikes

Russia and Ukraine held their first US-mediated talks in nine months in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November, the delegations confirmed an agreement in principle on a large prisoner exchange without a ceasefire, and strikes on five major substations kept emergency outages in force.

The first day, on 10 November, lasted four hours and was the first US-mediated session in nine months. Neither Secretary of State Rubio nor President Zelensky attended, and a second day was scheduled for 11 November, and it took place.34 On the evening of 10 November, United24, citing Interfax-Ukraine, and Kurs, citing Ukrainska Pravda, relayed a source close to the Ukrainian delegation saying a large-scale exchange had been agreed, with no official confirmation at the time.1819 Zelensky said formulas of 1,000 for 1,000 or 1,500 for 1,500 were under discussion, which is not an agreed figure.20 The two days of talks ended on 11 November with an agreement in principle on a large exchange. Russian lead negotiator Vladimir Medinsky and Ukrainian lead negotiator Rustem Umerov confirmed the planned exchange, Medinsky describing the agreement as “in principle”, and the exact numbers and timing were not announced.421 Meduza reported that the central topic was the European security system, with understanding on some points and not others, and that territory remained the hardest issue; the AP report described no major breakthrough, and no source reviewed reports an energy ceasefire.214 The exchange is a planned one and not yet a completed one.

On the grid, Kurs reported that Russia attacked ten regions on the evening of 9 November and overnight into 10 November and struck five major energy substations, including sites in the Poltava, Dnipropetrovsk, Vinnytsia and Kirovohrad regions.22 Power outages affected six regions on 10 November, with emergency outages in Kyiv and four other regions, according to UNN, and the energy ministry’s Nekrasov said the situation remained difficult in the Kyiv and Dnipropetrovsk regions.2324 Hourly outage schedules and capacity restrictions for industry were in effect on 10 November.23

For operators and investors with Ukrainian telecom, data-centre or tower assets, the talks left the grid exposure where it was, because substations were being struck while negotiators met, and battery autonomy, generator fuel and the rotation of outage groups set availability more than any headline from Abu Dhabi. For these assets, a verifiable halt to attacks on energy infrastructure would be more directly relevant to near-term availability than the announcement of a prisoner exchange.

Great Sea Interconnector and the East Med Cable Surveys

Greece issued NAVTEX 1411/26 on 6 November for Abeille Marengo surveys running to 6 March 2027, Turkey answered with counter-notices on 6 and 10 November, and the vessel sailed from Heraklion on 10 November.

Greece’s Heraklion station issued NAVTEX 1411/26 on 6 November for survey work by the French research vessel Abeille Marengo for the Great Sea Interconnector (GSI), the Greece-Cyprus power link, covering 6 November 2026 to 6 March 2027 from south-east Crete toward the east of Rhodes and the Dodecanese.25 Turkey replied within hours with a counter-notice claiming that part of the area lies on its continental shelf.26 On 10 November it issued NAVTEX 1127/26, which says the work follows lines agreed with Turkish authorities who have given consent, covers 10 November 2026 to 19 March 2027 and asks other vessels to keep two nautical miles away.2728 Greek Foreign Ministry sources called the Turkish notice irrelevant and said that only Greece and Cyprus had received requests for authorisation, and that no request was submitted to Turkey.29

The vessel left Heraklion on the afternoon of 10 November, with its first stop near Karpathos for equipment calibration, and Greek City Times reported that two French warships had reportedly sailed for the wider survey area to provide an escort, a detail that remains unconfirmed.30 None of the sources reviewed reports physical interference with the vessel.

A second front runs through the Cypriot exclusive economic zone. Cyprus issued a NAVTEX for geophysical surveys by the vessel Kobi Ruegg from 2 to 15 November for the East to Med Corridor (EMC), a telecom cable between Saudi Arabia and Greece, and Turkey’s Antalya station issued counter-NAVTEX 1100/26 on 6 November, in force to 16 November. Greece issued its own notice for Kobi Ruegg valid from 7 to 11 November in the Greek zone.3132 The dispute therefore touches a power interconnector and a telecom cable at the same time, and the current notices concern survey operations and do not show that cable laying has begun.

For owners, lenders and insurers of East Mediterranean routes, the live exposure is schedule and permit risk. Survey windows run to March 2027 and each notice can be answered by a counter-notice. An incomplete survey leaves route, construction-cost and schedule assumptions unresolved and may complicate insurance placement and financing. The sources show a legal and navigational dispute carried out through notices, with no incident reported, and underwriting files could be written with both a clean-survey case and a slipped-survey case.

Norway: Data-Centre Grid Reservations Paused

Statnett announced on 9 November that it was pausing new data-centre grid-capacity reservations and the processing of Section 14 technical approvals, with 800 MW connected, 4,200 MW reserved and 9,100 MW in the queue, and the reservation pause due to be lifted by 1 August 2027 at the latest.

Norway’s grid operator Statnett said on 9 November that it had suspended two things: new capacity reservations for data centres, and the processing of applications for approval of facility functionality under Section 14 of the regulations on system responsibility (FOS), which sets the system-technical requirements a facility must meet to connect.3334 Statnett expects to lift the reservation suspension by 1 August 2027 at the latest. Resumption of Section 14 approval processing depends on functional requirements for data centres being established and approved by the Norwegian Energy Regulatory Authority (RME) after consultation.3334 The pause is not a general halt: existing connections are unaffected, facilities with approved functional requirements can still connect, and Statnett will consider individual applications for smaller facilities planned to connect up to 1 August 2027.3334

The queue is large. About 800 MW of data-centre load is connected, 4,200 MW is reserved, 9,100 MW is in the queue, and requests for a further 18,000 MW were reported, against a national peak consumption of slightly over 25,000 MW.35 Digitalization Minister Torgeir Micaelsen said the government wants data centres but not every type, which signals a more selective policy approach, and the government plans a licensing regime, with the main principles due “in the autumn”; the legal briefings cited do not give a firm implementation timetable.3634

For developers and investors with Norwegian projects, the position depends on four things, in this order: whether the facility is an existing connection, whether it has approved functional requirements, whether it holds a reservation but still lacks technical approval, and whether it sits in the queue behind them. Reserved projects can still stall without Section 14 approval, and the possible requirements include staying connected during grid disturbances, limits on how fast consumption can change and measures against oscillations, so technical compliance cost is part of the underwriting.3334 Applications for direct connection to the 420 kV grid that were already received are also halted, while pending regional-grid applications with near-term commissioning are not.34 Statnett has said capacity that becomes available will not go to others at the expense of data centres ahead in the queue.34
